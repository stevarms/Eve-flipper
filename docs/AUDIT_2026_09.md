## 5. Accumulate and the Fuzzwork archive

Accumulate reads exactly two sources: `FetchRegionOrders` for a live book
snapshot, and `marketDerivedFor` for ESI daily history. Nothing outside the
importer and the backtest replay touches Fuzzwork data.

**That is correct for the price analysis.** Fuzzwork archives are *quotes* —
what was on offer — while Accumulate asks whether something is cheap against
what actually *traded*. ESI history is the right source, and the liquidity gate
already uses it (`pct.AvgDailyVolume` / `pct.AvgDailyISK`, `accumulate.go:368`),
not the live book. The live snapshot only prefilters candidates and supplies
today's entry price.

**One real gap it could close.** Entry is today's lowest *ask* — correct, you buy
from a sell order. Exit is `pct.P50`, the yearly median of the daily *average*,
net of fees only (`accumulate.go:388-390`). So the model buys at the ask and
sells at a mid-market number, and the bid-ask spread never appears as a cost.
The 12% minimum-upside gate probably absorbs it on liquid items, but it is
unmodelled rather than accounted for. Historical bid-ask spread per item is
exactly what ESI cannot provide and the Fuzzwork order-book archive can — the
one use for that data in Accumulate worth arguing for.

1. **Rewrite `AUDIT_INDEX.md` to match the code** *(hours)* — it is actively
   misleading: all five priority findings are fixed and its Phase 3 status is
   wrong in both directions. A stale audit is worse than none because it is
   trusted.
2. **Collapse the four hub lists into one**, in Go, served to the client
   *(hours)* — two are byte-identical, one silently omits Hek, one is regions.
3. **Delete the second navigation taxonomy** *(hours)* — drop
   `group: "scan" | "tools"` and give the Cockpit builder the workspace name.
   Two disagreeing classifications, one printed raw, is the concrete form of
   "the UI is a mess".
4. **Finish both NDJSON migrations** *(days)* — split `streamNdjson` into a
   line-level primitive so one-object streams stop re-implementing framing.
5. **Define a row-actions contract and apply it to all eight result views**
   *(days)* — decide once which verbs a row supports, then have each view
   declare which apply and render them identically. Highest-value consistency
   fix, and it makes consolidating the 20 bespoke tables worthwhile afterwards.
6. **Extract the backtest orchestration** *(hours)* — before a fourth model
   appears. The station maker model proves more will be added.
7. **Decide what 23 tabs are actually for** *(your call)* — nine in Trade is the
   grab-bag relocated. Some are genuinely separate jobs; Optimizer, Edge and Risk
   may be panels rather than tabs. Structure I can measure; which you actually
   open in a week only you know.
